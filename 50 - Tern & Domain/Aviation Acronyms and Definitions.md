## ACC – Area Control Centre

Control of air traffic that is reaching, leaving, or maintaining cruising flight. Usually from flight level 200 (FL200) and upwards, also known as Enroute ATC.

Defined as the stage of flight that begins at the end of a defined departure procedure, and ends at the beginning of a defined arrival procedure.

## APP – Approach Control

Responsible for the provision of Air Traffic Control (ATC) to departing and arriving traffic in the lower altitude sectors in the vicinity of an airport. Flights will take off, and shortly after departure be transferred from tower (TWR) to Approach Control (APP). In reverse order, the area control centre (ACC) will descend aircraft out of cruising flight and hand them over to APP.

## APW – Area Proximity Warning

APW analyses known airspace restrictions and compares it to surveillance data and predicted flight paths, to warn a controller when an aircraft is entering, or soon will enter, prohibited airspace. The ATM system must be updated with current airspace activations and restrictions (known as "volumes") to provide coherent APW warnings.

## ATCO – Air Traffic Control Officer

ATCOs are responsible for the safe and orderly flow of air traffic in the global air traffic control system. Usually stationed in air traffic control centers and control towers on the ground, they monitor the position, speed and altitude of aircraft in their assigned airspace visually and by radar, and communicate with the pilots by radio.

## AWOS – Automated Weather Observing System

A ground based system that automatically provides updated weather information to a user.

## CLAM – Cleared Level Adherence Monitoring

CLAM is a ground-based safety net that alerts the ATCO when the surveillance system receives altitude information that deviates from the cleared profile. For example, aircraft climbing/descending without a clearance, or maintaining a level when cleared to climb or descend.

## DSAM – Downlinked Selected Altitude Monitor

DSAM is a ground based safety net that alerts the ATCO to a discrepancy between the cleared flight level (FL) and the FL entered by the flight crew in the cockpit. DSAM is transmitted over the Mode S surveillance system. Also known as Selected Vertical Intent or Final State Selected Altitude, this safety net allows the control to correct errors made by the flight crew before they become serious, and eliminates a potential source of frequency congestion.

## FDPS – Flight Data Processing System

Current flight data system in use by Isavia. Used in conjunction with ISDS for flight and airspace management. Uses electronic flight strips.

(Originally developed by a Canadian company that went bankrupt. Then finalized and maintained by Isavia).

## FIC – Flight Information Centre

Provides Flight Information Service (FIS) to air traffic. This is not ATC, but a service provided to allow safe and efficient conduct of flight operations. The service includes, but is not limited to, weather information, availability and serviceability of radio nav aids, alerting service for search and rescue (SAR), operational status of airfields, information on balloons and other obstructions, information on volcanic activity. FIS can also provide information of possible conflicting traffic, but they do not provide a separation service - the information is advisory and it is the responsibility of the pilot to maintain separation. FIS is frequently used in uncontrolled airspace.

## Inter-centre Coordination

Coordination messages between different ATC units, e.g. Marseille ACC coordinating flights with Barcelona ACC, Reykjavík with Oslo etc. These messages must be passed in a standardized, computer-readable protocol such as OLDI.

## Inter-sector Coordination

Coordination messages between different ATC sectors within the same unit, i.e. running the same air traffic management (ATM) software. For example Budapest East sector (Polaris) sending messages to Budapest West sector (also on Polaris). It is useful if these messages conform to international standards, but because we control both ends of the message stream, the message type, format and interaction can be designed to suit the needs of the unit.

## ISDS – Integrated Situation Display System

Air situation display in use by Isavia (developed and maintained by Tern Customer Service). Displays surveillance tracks and correlates them to flight plans. However, clearances and interactions are delivered via the FDPS, not the radar screen of ISDS (that is though on the Isavia roadmap).

## LoA – Letter of Agreement

Negotiated agreement between two ATC units on the agreed rules and procedures for handling traffic flows. For example Bodo Oceanic and Reykjavík will have an agreed set of transfer conditions for traffic passing between them. This will cover flight level allocation schemes, coordination points, OLDI message timings and verbal coordination requirements and procedures.

## METAR – Meteorological Aerodrome Report

A text based format for reporting the current weather conditions in and around a specific airport. Report includes wind, visibility, cloud presence, temperature/dew point, pressure, and any significant short-term expected changes.

## MSAW – Minimum Safe Altitude Warning

MSAW is a ground based safety net that alerts the controller to risk of collision with high terrain or man-made obstacles. A map of minimum safe altitudes is overlaid upon the situation display, and MSAW compares aircraft transmitted altitude data with the local minimum safe altitude. If the aircraft is, or is soon predicted to be, below the applicable altitude, a warning is presented to the ATCO.

## MTCD – Medium Term Conflict Detection

A term with many interpretations. Controllers are excellent at predicting and solving conflicts in the 5-10 minute time window - but it is more difficult to accurately predict and evaluate traffic 15-20 minutes ahead. MTCD seeks to automate and simplify the scanning process by evaluating traffic scenarios and notifying the controller to potential conflicts, airspace intrusions, and complex traffic situations that will develop in the 15-25 minute time window. MTCD is a capability rather than a defined logic set or specification, many vendors have differing approaches and the industry has yet to settle on a provable, functional system that limits extraneous alerts. Many ATCOs only have experience in turning MTCD off, because it doesn't work well enough yet.

## NOTAM – Notice to Airmen / Notice to Air Mission

NOTAM is a notice containing information concerning the establishment, condition or change in any aeronautical facility, service, procedure or hazard, the timely knowledge of which is essential to personnel concerned with flight operations.

## OLDI – Online Data Interchange

OLDI is a protocol for the automatic exchange of flight data between ATC units. How does ATC Unit A tell ATC Unit B when to expect Flight XYZ123, for example? Either a controller can pick up the phone and do it manually, or a machine can send an automatic message in an agreed format, and the flight info is automatically updated on the receiver end. OLDI is one of the formats.

## RAM – Route Adherence Monitoring

RAM is a ground based safety net that alerts the controller to an aircraft deviating from its cleared lateral profile - e.g., an aircraft following a trajectory other than the cleared route. RAM alerts suspend trajectory calculation, as this depends on accurate information input that reflects the cleared profile.

## SFR – System Flight Record

Internal Polaris name for a flight plan, or any data that serves the purpose of a flight plan.

## SPECI – Special Forecast

A weather message format used when significant changes to a forecast have occurred or are expected to occur. Similar to METAR.

## STCA – Short Term Conflict Alert

A ground based safety net that alerts the ATCO of an imminent loss of separation. Typical look-ahead time is 2 minutes, and the alert will vary in severity based on distance/time parameters. The alert may also be configured to alert the ATCO when separation is not predicted to be lost, but will fall close to the minima.

## SUR – Surveillance

Any system or group of systems that produces real-time location information of air traffic.

## SYNOP – Synoptic Weather Observation

Another automated weather report tool. More detailed information than METAR, but reported every 3 hours, as opposed to METAR (every 15 minutes).

## TAMS – Tern ATS Message Switch

Router/switching system for Air Traffic Services messages regardless of protocol (AFTN/CIDIN/AMHS supported). TAMS maintains message traffic connectivity between Iceland and surrounding control areas. Currently in operation at Gufunes, Iceland but we have announced "end of life" and are not selling to others.

## TAS – Tern ATC System

Predecessor to Polaris, for Tower, APP and ACC. Air situation display (ASD) combined with Electronic Flight Strips.

## TWR – Tower Control

Provides ATC services to traffic on the ground (both aircraft and support vehicles), and airborne traffic in the immediate vicinity of the airport.
